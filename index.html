A worked example

A patient with a pre-existing respiratory condition, worsening breathing, travelling to the Caribbean on Sunday. They came in scared their holiday was going to be ruined.

Clinical note (Structured Consultation Output)

History

  • Face to face, seen alone, for a follow-up due to worsening breathing.
  • Reports worsening breathing, describing being prone to it due to a pre-existing illness. Intends to travel to the Caribbean, including Saint Lucia, Saint Kitts, Barbados, and the Dominican Republic.
  • Concerned about being able to enjoy the holiday due to current symptoms.
  • No mention of red flag symptoms.
  • Exercises regularly to manage symptoms but currently finds any exercise tiring.
  • PMH: existing condition prone to causing breathing issues.
  • DH: current medications include inhalers (Foster) and carbocisteine.
  • SH: plans to travel across the Atlantic to the Caribbean.

Examination

Mild wheeze and a few crackles noted on examination.

Diagnosis

1. Worsening breathing.

Plan

  • Treatment planned: prescribe antibiotics (twice a day for seven days) and a short course of oral steroids (once a day for five days).
  • Advice given: take antibiotics with breakfast and tea time, and steroids with breakfast, ideally with food. Can be taken alongside other medications.
  • Follow up plan: if not feeling better by their travel date on Sunday, book an appointment for the following Thursday or Friday to see the practitioner again.

Safety netting advice

If symptoms do not improve or worsen, advised to see the practitioner again.

Afternote (Patient Stream via CoLab Lens)

Your breathing and what to do before Sunday

You have been prescribed a short course of antibiotics and steroids to help improve your breathing over the next few days.

What to take

Antibiotics

Take twice a day for 7 days, with breakfast and your evening meal.

Steroids

Take once a day for 5 days, with breakfast. Take with food if possible.

Your usual inhalers and carbocisteine

Continue these as normal.

Before you travel: the aim of this treatment is to improve your breathing before your trip. If you are not improving by Sunday, contact the surgery before travelling.

Get medical help sooner if

  • your breathing gets worse
  • you feel short of breath at rest
  • you develop chest pain
  • you feel significantly more unwell, feverish, or confused
  • you are struggling to speak in full sentences

If symptoms are severe or sudden, seek urgent medical help.

What was left out, and why

This is one example from hundreds. We took anonymised clinical notes and argued — the four of us, nurse, counselling psychologist, social worker, applied philosopher — over what stays and what gets cut. From the common ground in those decisions, we built a rule set: the Lens. Every omission below reflects that rule set, not a one-off judgement call.

Mild wheeze and crackles

Examination findings. Written for the clinical record. The patient can't act on this and it would worry them without purpose.

Diagnosis: worsening breathing

They already know their breathing is worse. Labelling it adds nothing. The Afternote addresses the situation, not the classification.

Pre-existing condition detail

They've lived with this condition. They don't need it explained back to them at the end of a consultation.

Exercise currently tiring

They told the GP this themselves. Reflecting it back is noise. The Medication addresses it — the Afternote focuses on the medication.

Specific Caribbean destinations

Saint Lucia, Saint Kitts, Barbados, the Dominican Republic — clinically irrelevant detail. What matters to the patient is the single decision point: Sunday.

"Seen alone" and consultation framing

Administrative and record-keeping context. It has no bearing on what the patient needs to do next.
